## Week 11 Daily Writing Log: File Systems & Access Patterns

**When to complete:** Final 10 minutes of class (after labs) and extended reflection due with homework submission.

### In-Class Reflection (end of class)
Respond in 4–5 sentences while memories are fresh:
- Highlight one idea from today that will change how you organize or search for files.
- Cite a moment from lab (extension detective, access timing, or disk scheduling) that surprised you and explain why.
- Jot one real-world scenario where sequential access shines and one where direct access is essential.

### Extended Reflection (2–3 paragraphs, due with home lab)
Use the in-class notes plus the independent lab to guide your writing:
1. **Connect concepts to habits:** Describe one specific change you will make to your directory or backup routine based on what you learned about file systems and metadata.
2. **Analyze access patterns:** Compare sequential and direct access using an example from the lab. Include a sentence on how disk scheduling might affect your example on a busy system.
3. **Plan next steps:** Identify one question you still have about file storage or disk scheduling, and outline how you could investigate it (reading, experiment, office hours).

Turn in your reflection as plain text or PDF. Aim for clarity over length; tight, well-supported paragraphs beat long rambles.
