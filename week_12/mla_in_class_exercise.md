# Week 12 In-Class MLA Exercise

Purpose: Build a complete MLA-formatted page from scratch, practice citing three source types, and prepare to choose a research paper topic. All directions follow Purdue OWL's MLA Formatting and Style Guide.

## Learning Targets
- Configure a blank Word or Google Doc so it meets MLA layout expectations.
- Locate and record citation details for three different source types (book/ebook, scholarly article, credible website/media).
- Integrate at least two sources in a short paragraph using correct in-text citations.
- Produce a polished Works Cited page with hanging indents and alphabetical order.

## Materials
- Laptop with Microsoft Word **or** Google Docs (download as PDF or DOCX before submitting).
- Internet access to Purdue OWL (https://owl.purdue.edu/) and the CUNY Library databases/Google Scholar/news outlets.
- "4 Required Formal Writing Assignments scaffolding 10_20.pdf" for assignment context.

## Activity Steps
1. **Set up the document**
   - Open Word or Docs > set 1" margins, Times New Roman 12 pt, double-spacing, left alignment.
   - Insert header: `LastName Page#` flush right.
   - Type the MLA heading (your name, instructor, course, due date) and center a working title such as "MLA Practice Portfolio".

2. **Source Scavenger Hunt**
   - Locate one source of each type below. Record full citation info as you go.
     - **Book or ebook** (can be a chapter, anthology selection, or reference book).
     - **Scholarly article** from a database; download the PDF for annotation.
     - **Credible website or multimedia** (news article, government report, TED Talk, YouTube video from an expert channel).
   - Copy key metadata: author(s), title, container (journal/site), publisher, date, page range or URL, DOI if available.

3. **In-text citation practice**
   - Draft one paragraph (6-8 sentences) on the topic of your choice
   - Integrate at least **two** of your sources:
     - Use one **short quotation** with a signal phrase.
     - Paraphrase another idea and cite it parenthetically.
   - Follow Purdue OWL guidance for page numbers, punctuation, and hanging indents. Highlight citations so you can check them later.

4. **Build the Works Cited page**
   - Insert a page break and center the heading **Works Cited**.
   - Create MLA entries for all three sources. Use a hanging indent (0.5").
   - Alphabetize and remove live hyperlinks (Ctrl/Cmd + Shift + F9 in Word; Format > Clear Formatting in Docs).

5. **Peer or self-check**
   - Run through the Purdue OWL checklist: margins, fonts, spacing, header, heading, title, in-text citations, Works Cited formatting.
   - Confirm every in-text citation has a matching Works Cited entry.

6. **Export and share**
   - Word: File > Save As > PDF or DOCX. Docs: File > Download > PDF Document (.pdf) **and** keep an editable copy.
   - Upload to the LMS or share the link according to instructor instructions.

## Deliverables
1. One-page MLA practice document (PDF or DOCX) containing:
   - Properly formatted heading, title, and paragraph with two in-text citations.
   - A Works Cited page with at least three entries (book/ebook, scholarly article, credible website/media).
2. A short reflection comment in the LMS: Which MLA skill felt most comfortable? Which needs more practice?

## Quick Rubric (10 pts)
| Criterion | Description | Pts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MLA Layout | Margins, header, heading, title, spacing follow Purdue OWL | 2 |
| Source Variety | Three distinct, credible sources; metadata captured accurately | 2 |
| In-Text Citations | At least two citations formatted correctly with signal phrases/page numbers | 3 |
| Works Cited | Entries complete, alphabetical, hanging indents, punctuation per MLA | 2 |
| Reflection | Honest self-assessment posted in LMS | 1 |

## Extension / Early Finisher Options
- Add a block quote and format it using MLA conventions.
- Create a second paragraph that synthesizes a counterargument.
- Start outlining two potential research paper topics and list supporting sources for each.

## Troubleshooting Tips
- If a source has no author, begin the Works Cited entry with the title and use that in the in-text citation.
- Page numbers missing? Use paragraph numbers (par. 4) or section headings if the source provides them; otherwise omit.
- Purdue OWL has sample papers you can compare against--keep a tab open for quick checks.
